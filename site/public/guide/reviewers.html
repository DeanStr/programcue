Review assigned proposals

This is for evaluators. You score the proposals you were assigned. You do not publish the programme or release final decisions.

Accept the invitation

  1. Open the invitation from the event team.
  2. Sign in at app.programcue.com/sign-in with the same email, or create an account if you do not have one.
  3. Accept the invitation if Program Cue asks you to.

If you land on a page that says you have no workspace access, the invitation is missing, expired or was sent to a different email. Ask the committee chair to send it again.

Open the Review workbench

After you accept, Program Cue opens the Review workbench. You only see the proposals assigned to you. Use the list to move from one assignment to the next.

Answer the conflict question

Before you can submit, you must answer Conflict of interest. Choose No conflict if you can score this proposal impartially. Choose I have a conflict if you should not judge it. You can still save a draft before you answer.

Score a proposal

  1. Read the proposal on one side of the workbench. Fill in the scorecard on the other.
  2. Scores save as you go. The workbench tells you when a change is saving, saved, offline or needs retry. Refreshing brings back the last saved draft.
  3. Choose a Recommendation from the choices the organisers set for this round, and a Confidence from 1 to 5. Both are required.
  4. Applicant feedback can be sent to the person who wrote the proposal when organisers publish a decision. Private notes stay with the committee.
  5. If two tabs both have unsaved edits, Program Cue asks you to resolve the clash. It will not keep the later tab in silence.
  6. When the scorecard is complete and you have answered the conflict question, submit the review. That finishes this assignment and can open the next one.

If the event has turned on AI suggestions, they are labelled as suggestions. They are not your score until you accept them into the scorecard.

If the round is blind

You see only the answers the organisers marked as review content. Names, titles built from identifying details and other unmarked fields are hidden. Do not try to identify the applicant from what remains.

If you have a conflict

Choose I have a conflict, then Declare conflict and return. Scoring is disabled and nothing you typed is sent to the applicant. That recusal is recorded. Do not skip the assignment or score someone you should not judge.

What happens after you submit

The committee chair and organisers see your submitted score in Review & selection. After you submit, you can read and add to Committee discussion for that proposal. They release decisions. You will not see unpublished planning, other people’s assignments or the public programme controls.

Chairs who need to set up rounds and assignments should use Review and selection.
